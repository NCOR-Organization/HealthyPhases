# **Going through it Alone: A Cross‑sectional Study on the Prevalence of Loneliness among Medical, Nursing, and Paramedical Students in a Health Science Academy in Bangalore, India** 

## **Navya P. Mangali, Bobby Joseph, Merlyn Joseph, Vilas M. Tellis** 

Department of Community Medicine, St. John’s Medical College, Sarjapur Road, Koramangla, Bangalore, Karnataka, India 

## ~~**Abstract**~~ 

**Background:** Life of students enrolled in a medical academy is stressful and often demanding in various aspects. Frequently, such students tend to be lonely. The prevalence of this problem has not been assessed and has been given little attention in medical schools. The objectives of this study were to determine the prevalence of loneliness among medical, nursing, and paramedical undergraduate students in a Health Science Academy in Bangalore and to assess the factors associated with loneliness among the study population. **Materials and Methods:** This was a cross-sectional study conducted among 400 students of a Health Science Academy and the selection of the subjects was performed by stratified random sampling. Each student was given a self-administered questionnaire which comprised of two sections. Section 1 which included the sociodemographic details and Section 2 was the revised UCLA (University of California, Los Angeles) scale (Version 3). **Results:** Prevalence of loneliness among the students in the academy was found to be 52%. Factors associated with loneliness and possibly contributing to it were assessed using the Mann-Whitney U test and the Pearson Chi-square test. The factors that impacted loneliness significantly were language familiarity, academics, relationships with friends, and family, health problems, and involvement in physical activities. **Conclusion:** The prevalence of loneliness among the students was found to be 52%. There were various factors, but despite such worrisome scores, the health science academy still ranks high in surveys that assess personality development as part of college rankings in the country. Further research would be needed to know the exact direction of association between loneliness and the associated factors. 

**Keywords:** Loneliness, medical institute, solitude, stress, student

It has been shown that loneliness results in a decline in well-being and has an adverse effect on physical health.[[3]] High degrees of loneliness may even precipitate suicidal ideation.[[4]]

## **materIalS and methodS** 

## **Ethics approval** 

Approval was obtained from the Institutional Ethics Committee (IEC Study Reference NumberNo: 97/2019) and informed written consent from the students were obtained.

This was a cross-sectional study conducted among a total of 400 students belonging to a Health Science Academy. 

The Health Science Academy in Bangalore consists of a medical college, a nursing college, an institute for paramedical sciences, a research institute, and a tertiary care teaching hospital. 

## **Study period** 

The study was conducted over 1 year (June 2019 – June 2020).

## **Study instrument** 

The study instrument was a self-administered questionnaire which comprises of the following sections: 

Section 1 - Sociodemographic details including age, gender, languages spoken, state, and city/town/village of origin were recorded. 

Section 2 - The assessment of loneliness was performed using the revised UCLA scale (Version 3). It was developed by psychologist Daniel Russell (1996)[[5]] and its a 20-item measure that is used to assess how often a person feels disconnected from others. The subject was given the 20-item questionnaire in which there is a 4-point rating scale and the response format being 1 = never, 2 = rarely, 3 = sometimes, and 4 = always. Later, the positively worded items were reverse-coded so that high values meant more loneliness, and a score was calculated for each respondent by averaging their ratings. 

## **Sample size** 

The sample size was calculated by using the formula. 

n = Z[2] pq/d[2] 

Where n = the sample size. 

Z = 1.96, assuming an alpha level of 5%. 

p = estimated prevalence, chosen to be 50% since no previous studies estimating loneliness in a similar population were available. 

q = 100- _P_ = 50% 

d = Desired level of precision, chosen as 5% (absolute). 

Substituting, the sample size in this study is calculated as being: N = (1.962)[2 ] × (50 × 50)/(5 × 5) = 384 rounded off to 400 

## **Sampling technique** 

Stratified random sampling was used to select the subjects for the study. The steps were the following: 

- a. The sample size of 400 was allocated to each of the 4 streams proportionate to the total number of students in that stream. 

- b. Within each stream, the sub-sample for that stream was allocated to the different batches proportionate to the total number of students in each batch. 

- c. Within each group, the required number of students was identified by simple random sampling among the list of students belonging to that batch in that stream. 

Using this technique, the allocated sample sizes allocated to each stream were as follows: 

- MBBS students: 177 

- Nursing students: 105 

- Allied Health Sciences students: 118.

All data collected was entered into a MS Excel sheet and subjected to analysis using standard statistical packages. Loneliness was expressed as a mean score across categories. ## **dIScuSSIon** 

Our study found that 52% of students experienced loneliness. Similar studies in other universities around the world have reported varying prevalence rates: 60.2% in Turkey,[[6]] 49.5% in Ethiopia,[[7]] and 26.4% in Iran.[[8]] These variations may be because of differences in sample size, cultural, economic, and social factors. 

The factors that were found to be associated with loneliness were mainly ones revolving around college life, family life, academics, social life, and interpersonal relationships with friends and family, health, and involvement in physical activities. 

In college life [Table 2], familiarity with the local language impacts loneliness, enabling effective communication with peers and professors. Feeling ignored ( _P_ < 0.001) may stem from language barriers, contributing to difficulties in social interaction. Social control correlates with reduced loneliness and decreased anxiety levels.[[9]] Our study found lower loneliness scores among students fluent in the local language. Peer pressure was also linked to loneliness ( _P_ < 0.001) pushing students into uncomfortable situations or habits. Social life [Table 3] also impacts loneliness. Meeting friends in Bangalore ( _P_ < 0.001) reduced loneliness scores, highlighting that interpersonal relationships foster a sense of belonging. Higher social expressivity is linked to lower loneliness levels.[[9]] Dissatisfaction with friendships ( _P_ < 0.001) leads to distrust and social skill hindrance, leading to isolation. Relationship between the use of social media and loneliness with low self-esteem have been established in young people.[[10,11]] In our study too, students more engaged in social media felt lonelier ( _P_ < 0.01), seeking support from alternative sources while struggling with college and friendships. 

Academics also play a crucial role. [Table 4]. Health academies have a challenging syllabus and poor academic performance correlates with higher loneliness and depression risk.[[12,13]] We found that unsatisfactory grades ( _P_ < 0.001) contribute to loneliness, potentially because of pressure from family or teachers, or difficulty understanding the format of writing an answer despite studying. Exam stress ( _P_ < 0.001) exacerbates loneliness, causing anxiety, fear and withdrawal from extracurricular, and social activities, increasing isolation. 

Various parameters of family life [Table 5] were associated positively with loneliness. Students who didn’t visit nearby relatives were lonelier ( _P_ < 0.005) than ones who did. Poor family relationships strongly predicted loneliness, as lack of meaningful ties fosters seclusion. This is consistent with previous research findings indicating that positive family functioning, defined by frequent positive interactions between parents and children and minimal parental stress, contribute to better emotional well-being[[14]] family crises also increased loneliness ( _P_ < 0.05), causing withdrawal from social activities because of traumatic memories, consistent with findings that adverse childhood experiences affect adolescent health and well-being.[[14]] 

Being physically active was negatively associated with loneliness among the students of this college. Page _et al_ . and Diehl K _et al_ . both discovered that university students who engaged in little to no physical activity reported higher levels of loneliness.[[4,15]] Along with lack of physical activity ( _P_ < 0.005), we also found that poor psychological health ( _P_ < 0.001) [Table 6] was also found to be associated with increased loneliness. Students who lacked time for hobbies felt alone ( _P_ < 0.001). Neglecting self-care can lead to mental fatigue, disinterest in activities, and withdrawal from conversations. This fatigue, both mental and physical, may contribute to loneliness. Although the precise direction of causation remains unclear, there is a clear association between these factors. 

It was also identified that “emotional loneliness” was more common than “social loneliness,” both phenomena being positively associated with feelings of depression and anxiety.[[5]] It is fair to say that social and demographic changes have led to an increased prevalence of loneliness and social isolation in modern society.[[16]] 

No correlation was found between loneliness and sex in our study. Previous studies across various age groups have yielded inconsistent findings, with some studies indicating higher loneliness among women,[[17,18]] others suggesting higher loneliness in men,[[19,20]] and some finding no significant sex differences.[[21,22]] 

Loneliness has been found to be associated with an unhealthy lifestyle and poorer health.[[23]] With respect to mental health, loneliness has been proven to be associated with suicidal and parasuicidal ideation, with the prevalence of this ideation being directly proportional to the degree of loneliness.[[3]] It also increases the likelihood to eventually develop clinical depression.[[24]] Other long-term health impacts due to loneliness are increased risk of hypertension,[[25]] coronary heart disease,[[26]] and metabolic syndrome.[[27]] It is also a risk factor for physical inactivity and escalates the probability that physical activity will be stopped over time.[[28]] Loneliness was linked with

Despite such worrisome scores of loneliness, the health science academy where this study was conducted continues to excel in national college rankings for personality development. It was ranked 17[th] among medical colleges in India in 2020 by India Today, 13[th] by the week in 2019, and 4[th] in India by outlook India in 2019. The study can have several implications for the institution, the students, and the healthcare profession on the whole. 

The students: Awareness of this problem will increase among the student community and they will be encouraged to work on this using peer-support strategies. 

The Institution: The results will be presented to the management to ensure that a program is put in place for the welfare of the students. This could include workshops, mentorship and others. 

The Medical/Social profession: Results of this study, when placed in the public domain could help fill gaps in the literature regarding the problem of loneliness in students, and possible means to address the problem. This study is currently envisaged at one institution but could lead to it being replicated across many institutions, health, and nonhealth. 

## **Recommendations** 

## _For students_ 

College life is a demanding one but the key is learning how to deal with the problems coming one’s way. In college, learning the language and local culture aids adaptation. Building meaningful relationships with friends and family is also crucial. Staying in touch, even if not through long phone calls, but through simple text messages could eliminate any feeling of loneliness. 

## _For the institution_ 

Each college should ensure that students realize the value of effective communication. More activities should be included in the curriculum where interaction with classmates will be inevitable. 

## _For the medical profession_ 

Such studies should be encouraged to understand the problems faced by students. Identifying and tackling the problem will help future healthcare workers to learn their skills in a healthy environment and this will in turn reflect in the quality of care provided by them. 

## **Limitations of the study** 

The study design is a cross-sectional study and the results obtained were only the factors associated with loneliness. Temporal association between loneliness and the factors could not be established. Prospective studies in the future can help to overcome this limitation. 

We were also unable to get documentary evidence of previous history of psychiatric or neurological disorders. In addition, history of intake of drugs (which could affect mental health) was not established. 

This is a single-center study. This may not reflect the true loneliness levels in all health science academies as a whole. 

## **concluSIon** 

Various steps must be taken to ensure a pleasant and healthy environment for students to pursue their careers. They should be educated on the small steps they themselves can take to reduce the incidence of loneliness. The college should also take the necessary actions needed. Further studies should be performed to determine the exact direction in which each parameter is related to loneliness using a prospective study.
