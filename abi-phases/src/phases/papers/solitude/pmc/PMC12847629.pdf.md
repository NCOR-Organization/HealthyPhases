## One hundred trials of solitude: A call to action 

## _To the Editor:_ 

In 1967, Colombian author Gabriel García Marquez[�] published _One Hundred Years of Solitude_ , a masterpiece of magical realism. The novel follows the Buendía family over the course of a century in the fictional town of Macondo. Through a series of triumphs, tragedies, natural disasters, civil wars, and magical events, Jose[�] Arcadio Buendía, his wife Úrsula Iguaran,[�] and their descendants experience remarkable success and eventual decline. 

In many ways, the evolution of the Australian and New Zealand Intensive Care Society Clinical Trials Group mirrors that of the Buendía family and the growth of Macondo. Since its founding in 1994, the group has flourished, thanks in large part to visionaries like Professor Rinaldo Bellomo, whose pioneering efforts laid the foundation for our community’s success. As his colleagues and successors, we are the inheritors of that legacy, overcoming challenges and celebrating remarkable achievements together. 

Though we mourn the loss of Professor Bellomo, these are exciting times for intensive care research in Australia, New Zealand, and around the world. We are fortunate to have a wealth of world-leading clinician-researchers, and we continue to support both established and emerging investigators from a variety of disciplines. Our flexible network of researchers, coordinators, and project managers, supported by three premier methodological centres―the Australian New Zealand Intensive Care Research Centre, the Medical Research Institute of New Zealand, and the George Institute for Global Health―enables us to conduct highquality, patient-centred clinical trials. Despite facing fiscal and logistical challenges, we remain committed to pushing the boundaries of critical care research design and implementation. 

To honour Professor Bellomo's legacy and avoid the fate of Macondo, we must remain vigilant. We must confront both 

present and emerging challenges, taking proactive steps to safeguard our community―our Macondo―ensuring that it thrives for generations to come.
