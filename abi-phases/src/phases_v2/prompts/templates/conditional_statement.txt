You are extracting CONDITIONAL STATEMENTS (claim-like axioms) from a research text.

Goal: capture what the paper is asserting as a general rule, mechanism, or
conditional relationship — the kind of conclusion a careful reader would draw
from this passage. The output will be used to search across many papers for
papers that say specific things.

What to extract
- General, self-contained statements that express a relationship, rule,
  mechanism, condition, or consequence.
- Every sentence MUST use the form:
    "If <X>, then <Y>."
  No other phrasing is allowed. If a claim does not fit this form, rephrase it
  until it does, or drop it.
- Each sentence must stand on its own, with no pronouns referring to the
  surrounding text ("this", "these", "the authors", "the study"). Resolve
  references to concrete concepts.
- <X> and <Y> must EACH be understandable on their own: the two halves are
  also stored and compared separately. Name the subject explicitly in both
  halves. <Y> must never lean on a pronoun or back-reference to <X> ("they",
  "it", "their", "that child", "these individuals", "this"); repeat the noun
  phrase from <X> instead.
    Bad:  "If children are socially withdrawn, then they tend to be rejected
           by peers."
    Good: "If children are socially withdrawn, then socially withdrawn
           children tend to be rejected by peers."

Hard requirements
- Do NOT copy sentences verbatim from the text — rephrase as general claims.
- Do NOT invent facts that are not supported by the text.
- Do NOT include study metadata ("the paper finds...", "the authors argue...").
  State the claim directly as a general proposition.
- Use hedging ("may", "can", "often", "tends to") when the text itself hedges.
- Keep each sentence short (one clause + one consequence is ideal).
- Output 0 to 10 sentences. If the text contains no general claim, return an
  empty list.

Return strict JSON only in this exact shape:
{"conditional_statement": ["If ..., then ...", "If ..., then ...", "..."]}
