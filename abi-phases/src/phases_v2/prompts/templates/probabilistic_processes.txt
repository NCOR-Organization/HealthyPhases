Extract effect claims supported by the research text. Keep generic process
names and preserve the source qualification separately from the target effect.
Do not calculate a net sign or invert a claim.

Return {"relations": [...]} with 0 to 8 objects, each containing exactly:
- subject_process: the generic source process name.
- subject_participant: the noun phrase for the independent continuant that
  participates in the source process and bears the claimed disposition.
- subject_change: "none", "increases", "decreases", "no-effect",
  "prevents-increase", or "prevents-decrease".
- target_process: the generic target process name.
- direction: "increases", "decreases", "no-effect", "prevents-increase",
  or "prevents-decrease". This is the effect on the target, stated ONCE.
- evidence_text: one contiguous, verbatim passage from the supplied text,
  at most 2000 characters. Do not insert ellipses, repair wording, or paraphrase.

Meaning of the fields
- subject_change describes ONLY the source qualification explicitly stated:
  increased/higher/more source = increases; reduced/lower/less source = decreases.
  Use none when no source change is stated. None is NOT no-effect.
  Source prevention labels apply only if prevention on the source itself is
  explicitly what influences the target. Do not invent a source change.
- direction describes the target effect. Increases/decreases can concern the
  amount, likelihood, frequency, intensity, or duration of the target process;
  preserve the paper's qualification in the evidence rather than invent a metric.
- prevents-increase means stopping the target from increasing, NOT decreasing it.
- prevents-decrease means stopping the target from decreasing, NOT increasing it.
- no-effect requires an explicit claim of no effect. A nonsignificant result,
  "does not increase", "does not decrease", or missing evidence is not enough.
- Do not output target_change: that would encode the target effect twice.
- Neither a reduced source nor a prevention label ever flips direction.

Naming
Use plain processes such as "social support", "stress", "depression",
"social interaction", and "laughter". Keep meaningful context such as
"laughter with friends" versus "laughter alone"; do not collapse these into
laughter causing itself. Both endpoints should describe what unfolds, not an
anatomical location, test instrument, or paper/study.
Remove amount words from the names: "increased", "decreased", "reduced",
"elevated", "higher", "lower", "more", "less", "lack of".
Do not introduce wrappers such as "risk of", "onset of", "occurrence of",
"experiencing", "improvement in", or "reduction in" into a generic name.
"experiencing stress" -> "stress"
"reduced social support" -> "social support"
Do not remove the identity of a process: "lung volume reduction surgery" is
an intervention name, not a source decrease. Do not strip clinically meaningful
qualifiers such as "high-arousal negative emotions" merely because they say high.

Examples (field order: source, source change, target effect, target)
"Support reduces stress."
  social support | none | decreases | stress
"Reduced social support increases stress."
  social support | decreases | increases | stress
"More social support was associated with less negative affect."
  social support | increases | decreases | negative affect
"Less social support was associated with more negative affect."
  social support | decreases | increases | negative affect
"Support prevents an increase in stress."
  social support | none | prevents-increase | stress
"Support prevents a decrease in stress."
  social support | none | prevents-decrease | stress
"Preventing a decrease in social support reduces stress."
  social support | prevents-decrease | decreases | stress
"Preventing an increase in stress improves sleep."
  stress | prevents-increase | increases | sleep
"Support does not increase stress."
  OMIT: no increase does not establish no effect, decrease, or prevention.
"The intervention inhibited the reduction of stress."
  OMIT if the text does not establish prevention; weaker inhibition is ambiguous.

Grounding
- Extract asserted effects or explicitly signed associations. Do not upgrade
  association to proof of causation; preserve the associative wording in evidence.
- For an association, keep the predictor/source orientation stated by the text.
  Do not infer direction from "associated with" or "significant" alone.
- A symptom or diagnostic test detecting a condition does not cause that condition.
- Do not infer effects from reference titles, fictional narratives, hypotheses,
  proposed mechanisms without a supported claim, or descriptions of procedures.
- Preserve the distinction between source and target; do not swap them to fit.
- If the two generic processes and these fields cannot preserve the claim
  (e.g. an essential threshold, nested negation, or unclear direction), OMIT it.
  An empty list is preferable to an invented or inverted relationship.
