You are extracting EFFECT CLAIMS from a research text and rephrasing them in
BFO process form.

Goal: capture claims of the form

    "<subject> increases (or decreases, or has no effect on) <target>".

"Increases" means there is more of the target process: it becomes more
likely, more frequent, more intense, or lasts longer. "Decreases" means
there is less of it.

The output is consumed by an ontology where both endpoints must be BFO
processes (occurrents — things that unfold in time) and the subject process
must have an explicit independent-continuant participant that bears the
disposition.

What to extract
- Only claims where the text asserts (or strongly implies) a directional
  influence of one process on another.
- Each claim becomes ONE JSON object with these fields:

    {
      "subject_process":    "<plain name of the process>",
      "subject_participant": "<noun phrase: the bearer of the disposition>",
      "subject_change":     "none" | "more" | "less",
      "target_process":     "<plain name of the process>",
      "target_change":      "none" | "more" | "less",
      "direction":          "increases" | "decreases" | "no-effect",
      "evidence_text":      "<short verbatim quote from the text>"
    }

Hard requirements on shape
- `subject_process` and `target_process` MUST be processes — things that
  unfold in time.
  Examples (good): "sugar consumption", "social isolation", "regular
  meditation practice", "stress", "loneliness", "headache", "depression".
- When the text names a thing rather than a process, name the process it
  takes part in: "sugar" -> "sugar consumption".
- When the text already names a process, use its plain name. Do not wrap it
  in a phase or occurrence such as "onset of", "occurrence of",
  "episode of", "experiencing", "worsening of", "recovery from". The claim
  is that the subject increases, decreases, or has no effect on the process
  itself.
  Examples (bad — fix them): "onset of depression" -> "depression";
                              "occurrence of headache" -> "headache";
                              "experiencing loneliness" -> "loneliness".
- `subject_process` and `target_process` MUST NOT say which way something
  changes, how much of it there is, or how it compares. Leave out words
  such as "increased", "increasing", "decreased", "decreasing", "reduced",
  "elevated", "higher", "lower", "more", "less", "greater", and wrappers
  such as "risk of", "likelihood of", "probability of", "chance of".
  Level words ("high", "low", "poor", "lack of") follow the same rule,
  unless they are part of the established name of a condition
  (e.g. "high blood pressure"; better still, "hypertension").
- `subject_change` and `target_change` record what the text says about the
  amount of each process, which the names leave out:
    "none" — the text talks about the process itself;
    "more" — it talks about more of it ("increased", "higher", "elevated");
    "less" — it talks about less of it or its absence ("reduced", "lower",
             "lack of", "loss of", "poor", "absence of").
- `subject_participant` MUST be a noun phrase naming the independent
  continuant that participates in the subject process and bears the
  disposition. It is what the disposition "lives in".
  Example: subject_process="sugar consumption" -> subject_participant="sugar".
           subject_process="regular meditation practice" ->
                                       subject_participant="meditation practitioner".
- `direction` MUST be exactly one of: "increases", "decreases", "no-effect",
  and MUST be the direction the text states. Do NOT flip it to make up for
  a "less"; that is done later from the change fields.
  Use "no-effect" ONLY when the text explicitly asserts a null finding
  (e.g. "X had no significant effect on Y").
  Examples (bad — fix them):
    "increased stress" increases "depression"
        -> subject_process="stress", subject_change="more",
           direction="increases",
           target_process="depression", target_change="none"
    "regular meditation practice" decreases "increased stress"
        -> subject_process="regular meditation practice", subject_change="none",
           direction="decreases",
           target_process="stress", target_change="more"
    "sugar consumption" increases "risk of diabetes"
        -> subject_process="sugar consumption", subject_change="none",
           direction="increases",
           target_process="diabetes", target_change="none"
    "reduced social support" increases "depression"
        -> subject_process="social support", subject_change="less",
           direction="increases",
           target_process="depression", target_change="none"
- `evidence_text` MUST be a short verbatim quote (<= 200 characters) from
  the text supporting the claim. Do not paraphrase here.

Hard requirements on content
- Do NOT invent claims that are not supported by the text.
- Do NOT extract claims about the study itself (e.g. "the authors find...").
  State the claim as a general proposition about the world.
- If the direction is uncertain or hedged so heavily that you cannot pick
  one of the three options, DROP the claim entirely.
- Use the most specific phrasing the text supports.
- Output 0 to 8 claims. If the text contains no such claim, return an
  empty list.

Return strict JSON only in this exact shape:
{"relations": [
   {"subject_process": "...", "subject_participant": "...",
     "subject_change": "none",
     "target_process": "...", "target_change": "none",
     "direction": "increases",
     "evidence_text": "..."}
]}
